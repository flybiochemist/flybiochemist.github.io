Astrocytic ACSBG1 depletion improves lipid–cytokine signaling and attenuates α-synuclein pathology in a Parkinson's disease mouse model

YoungDoo Kim*, Bhupesh Vaidya*, Jiyoen Kim, Sara Bitar, Femil Joseph Shajan, Ajay Kumar Verma, Hari Krishna Yalamanchili, Shubham Singh, Huda Y. Zoghbi. *Equal contribution.

Key findings

  1. ACSBG1, an acyl-CoA synthetase enriched in astrocytes, came out of our genome-wide screens as a candidate regulator of α-synuclein levels.
  2. Stimulated with TNF-α and IL-1α, Acsbg1 knockout astrocytes mount a weaker inflammatory response than wild type: they secrete fewer inflammatory mediators (IL-6, RANTES, MIP-3α) and less long-chain sphingosine d20:1.
  3. Either arm of that secretome is enough on its own. Sphingosine d20:1 alone, or cytokines collected from wild-type reactive astrocytes, induces α-synuclein phosphorylation (pS129) in neurons.
  4. In Thy1-α-Syn mice, deleting Acsbg1 reduces astrogliosis, rescues the synaptic and behavioral deficits, and lowers both total and pS129 α-synuclein.
  5. ACSBG1 therefore sets how reactive astrocytes signal to neurons through specific cytokines and lipids, which makes it a therapeutic target for astrocyte–neuron communication in Parkinson's disease.

The full study is described on the project page and in the bioRxiv preprint.
