At a recent lab meeting, a postdoc raised a question: is WTC11 — the most widely used induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) line — really the same cell line as the WTC11 subline into which dCas9 has been inserted to make knockouts easy? They ran RNA-seq on both and showed how far apart the two had drifted. Differentiate each into neurons and the gap widens further, unsurprisingly. Presumably a great deal changed as the Cas9 construct went in, a single colony was picked, and the population was expanded back up from that one clone.

At least RNA-seq is easy and standardized, even if it costs money — most other assays are not. Most of the time it makes no dramatic difference which of the two lines you use, but if the drift happens to touch your particular gene, your experiment can fail because of it and you would never know.

Unless a cell line comes straight from ATCC or the lab that originally made it, you should assume it has drifted and plan accordingly. The hard part of biology is standardization, and standardization is a problem that stays hard until wet-lab work itself is standardized and automated.
