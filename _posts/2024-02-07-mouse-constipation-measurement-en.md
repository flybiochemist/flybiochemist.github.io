In neurodegenerative disease, Alzheimer's and Parkinson's included, reduced gut motility sometimes brings constipation along with it. Slower gut motility means food stays in the intestine longer, which means the gut over-absorbs water — that is the constipation — and it shows up in the brain-disease model mice too.

The simplest way to measure whether these mice are constipated: collect the morning poop, weigh it, bake it bone-dry in an oven, weigh it again, and use the weight difference to calculate the water content inside (constipation means less water in the stool). So every morning, the moment a mouse produces a fresh, warm dropping, I pick it up with forceps, seal it in a container, and weigh it...

You would think science means doing something grand, but the details are, well, this. And on top of it, I was somehow expecting a real difference in stool weight and there wasn't one — which is its own small disappointment.

Who knew a post about poop would do this well... maybe next time I need a different poop story.
