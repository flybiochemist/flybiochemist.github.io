The fun papers our lab's journal club is covering this week.

1. Making antibodies by vaccination takes a long time, and purifying and injecting them is expensive. So what about a transgenic parasite that makes the antibody itself, triggers no immune response, starts producing antibody immediately after you're infected, even self-propagates, and can be cleared with a pill once you no longer need it (the antibody gene, that is)?

2. Is your Threads posting habitual? Or does the reward system push you to post harder based on how many likes you get? A model predicting future posting volume from past like-counts, broken down by sex and age.

3. An agent-based system using a DeepSeek-based AI to help classify and diagnose rare diseases (DeepRare).

## Autism, non-coding RNA, and intercellular material transfer

4. Autism, 2-5 times more common in males, has driven a lot of X-chromosome research. But the protein-coding genes there have all been picked over already. Now it's the era of non-coding RNA — RNA that doesn't make protein but represses other genes. Starting from a non-coding RNA elevated in autism patients, the paper reconsiders the function of genome regions long assumed to be doing nothing.

5. Ever heard of trogocytosis? Think of it as cancer cells and immune cells, in direct contact, tearing off a large bite of membrane — cytoplasm included — and transferring it between cells. There's an attempt to engineer this mechanism to deliver large molecules: it doesn't travel as far as an extracellular vesicle, but it can deliver a lot more at once.
