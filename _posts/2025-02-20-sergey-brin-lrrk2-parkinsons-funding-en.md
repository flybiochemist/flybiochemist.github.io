In 2008, Google founder Sergey Brin learned that he had inherited the LRRK2-G2019S mutation from his mother. Someone carrying that gene has roughly a 20–45% chance of developing Parkinson's. Against 0.5–1.5% for the general population, that is far higher. But at that point, while symptoms could be eased, there was no way to treat it. So what do you do?

He dropped a funding bomb through one of the largest Parkinson's research foundations, the Michael J. Fox Foundation (yes, the man from Back to the Future), under the name Aligning Science Across Parkinson's (ASAP). He has given or committed $2 billion and plans to keep funding it. Set against the $250 million the NIH spends annually on Parkinson's research, that is already eight years of funding.

If Parkinson's research lately seems to be running at double speed, it is because the money really is doubled.
