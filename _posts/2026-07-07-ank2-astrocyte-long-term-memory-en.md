Lately a lot of the papers on astrocytes and memory are coming from Korean researchers and Korean labs. Memory has always been treated as something that happens inside neurons, but the functions of the surrounding glia — astrocytes and microglia — are now getting attention too. Microglia act by eating and eliminating synapses; astrocytes, through direct contact with neurons, are increasingly shown to help maintain neurons and the signalling inside them.

The kick in this paper is the dissociation. Reducing Ank2 expression in neurons had no effect on memory. Reducing it only in astrocytes left short-term memory intact but broke the maintenance of long-term memory. The authors then showed that calcium signalling was disrupted, through several rescue experiments (BDNF and downstream TrkB signalling).

## Why Ank2 of all genes

I am curious why Ank2 was picked out of all the possible genes, though papers often say nothing about the screening step that led them there.

I would also like to know what Ank2 levels look like in actual dementia patients with memory impairment, and in other conditions. But that is me speaking as a neurodegeneration researcher — for the paper's own aim, studying the short-to-long-term transition of memory within the hippocampus and the role of astrocytes in it, the choice fits cleanly.

This is work from Dr. Woo-Hyun Ko at IBS, and I look forward to what follows.
