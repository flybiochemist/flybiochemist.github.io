A few characteristics of Parkinson's-related genes, learned from a seminar by Professor Christine Klein of the University of Lübeck.

1. Parkinson's caused by a single gene accounts for about 15%. Most of it is presumably environmental factors plus a combination of many susceptibility genes.

2. Eighty per cent of Parkinson's research samples come from Caucasians, and 20% of those from Ashkenazi Jews. This is because of the LRRK2-G2019S mutation discussed below — which means that in other populations that mutation is rare or absent and different mutations in different genes stand out instead.

3. The LRRK2 G2019S mutation given as the example is carried by under 1% in the United States, but by 15% of the Israeli population and over 30% in North Africa. In China, by contrast, cases are barely reported. So European research (with much North African immigration) and American research (with a large Jewish population) were bound to study this mutation heavily.

4. Men are more likely to develop Parkinson's, and apparently there are marked sex differences in the tissue-to-tissue spread of Lewy bodies and in whether loss of smell progresses to Parkinson's.

5. The effective preventives for Parkinson's are smoking (!) and black tea (!!). Doing both together delayed onset by more than ten years in LRRK2-G2019S patients. Nobody knows which component, of course. Conversely, in the group consuming a lot of caffeinated soft drinks (energy drinks?), onset came ten years earlier. My own view is that this is because life is hard for people who drink energy drinks.

6. We need research on which combinations bring on Parkinson's in the 85% who have no individual causal mutation. My personal guess is that the way forward will be patient iPSC-organoids, or technology allowing many genes to be edited at once so that knock-in mice carrying all the mutations can be made. It also struck me that research tailored to people living in Korea is needed.
