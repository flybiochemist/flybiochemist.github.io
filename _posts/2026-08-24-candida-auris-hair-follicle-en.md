Candida auris, a multidrug-resistant yeast, travels down the hair shaft into the follicle, where it suppresses the initial cellular immune response and gets an infection established. While related yeasts are typically cleared by the immune system within about five days, this one keeps growing inside the follicle even after 30 days, weakens the skin barrier, and thereby invites more severe secondary infection.

This paper can be tough going for a non-specialist, but the moment that jolts you fully awake is the scene where the authors, trying to work out whether the hair itself or the follicle root is the actual problem, do not stop at shaving the mice — they remove the hair follicles outright before running the infection experiment. Shaving alone lowers the odds of infection somewhat; removing the follicle root raises the odds of blocking infection much further.

Reach total hairlessness and your odds of defending yourself against this particular yeast go up — so I suppose sometimes you gain something precisely by losing something.
