A fun Brunch page and paper I stumbled across while making slides on the history of Parkinson's.

## From Ayurveda to DBS

1. India's Ayurveda (500 BC) prescribed a certain plant root for Parkinson's patients, and it turns out that root contains about 5% of the very dopamine precursor still prescribed as medicine today. (Why is this actually true?)

2. In 1817, the physician James Parkinson fully catalogued the symptoms of the disease based on four of his own patients and two more he happened to observe on the street.

3. On hearing that a Parkinson's patient's symptoms improved after horseback riding, doctors of the time tried fitting patients with motorised helmets as a cure.

4. And yet deep brain stimulation (DBS) really does work well for Parkinson's and many other neurological conditions. (Why is this one actually true, too?)

5. (No clear rationale, just a hunch that it might work) During various brain surgeries, the most effective method discovered — by accident — was cutting a particular artery in the brain, the anterior choroidal artery, and reattaching it to a different vessel, which improved tremor on the opposite side of the body. Nobody knows why to this day, and with a 10%(!) mortality rate, it is no longer performed.

6. Since Parkinson's arises because dopamine-producing cells in the brain die and produce less dopamine, the argument went that anything that pumps out dopamine should help — and in drug trials, plenty of people given the placebo showed major symptomatic improvement anyway. The ultimate placebo effect (and, apparently, a recurring hazard for Parkinson's drug development, since the control arm keeps performing well).
