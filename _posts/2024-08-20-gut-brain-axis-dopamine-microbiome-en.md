Parkinson's presents through symptoms — trembling hands, stiffened movement, a stooped upper body, small steps and unsteady walking that leads to falls — but the cause is not in the muscles. Dopamine-releasing neurons in the dark region of the brain called the substantia nigra die off, dopamine release falls, and so every time the amount of dopamine in the brain fluctuates, muscular movement becomes hard to control.

This dopamine is also made in the gut. There are reports that the amount is comparable to or greater than what is made in the brain. But the brain is walled off very tightly so that no outside substance can enter (the blood-brain barrier), and while L-DOPA, the precursor from which dopamine is made inside cells, crosses that barrier, the finished dopamine does not. So L-DOPA is used as a drug, while boosting dopamine production in the gut has not become a treatment.

## Indirect protection through the vagus nerve

Even though gut dopamine cannot enter the brain directly, it does play one supporting role: activating the vagus nerve that connects gut and brain. The vagus nerve links the organs throughout the body to the brain, and dopamine produced in the gut raises its activity, causing more release of growth factors such as BDNF and thereby helping dopaminergic neurons die less. Not to the level of a treatment, of course.

Gut bacteria play an important part in producing this dopamine, and from the Parkinson's standpoint they are also involved with fatty acids, which matter a great deal. They break down dietary fibre that we generally cannot digest into short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), and these serve as an energy source for the gut wall, maintain its condition, and influence immune tolerance — gut and adipose tissue are enormously important in immunity.

## Why gut health connects to brain health

These SCFAs have a further function in protecting dopaminergic cells in the brain. So if the gut flora shift toward an environment full of unhelpful bacteria for whatever reason — drinking, eating food low in fibre — the SCFA composition changes, and along with gut health and immunity, dopamine is affected too.

Gut health is therefore connected to brain health. Not by spraying dopamine directly onto the brain, but because what is made in the gut affects the brain through various indirect routes.
